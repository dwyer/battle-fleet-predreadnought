    Choose Your Fleet's Heading

    Both fleets start facing each other from opposite sides of the board. If you and the enemy admiral choose the same heading, a contested approach occurs — the loser is caught 2/3 across the board with the winner bearing down from behind.

    Captain's Manual

    Phases (per round)

    Movement → Firing → Special Orders, each round. Each ship moves once per Movement phase, and that same phase is where you choose each ship's turret target (see Turret Targeting below). If you have nothing to shoot at, Skip Firing Phase takes you straight to the Special Orders phase (the enemy still fires). In the Special Orders phase, each ship picks exactly ONE action: a Torpedo Attack, a Lay Mine, or a Course Adjustment (a heading change up to half its turning stat, in place). Torpedoes launched this way strike at the end of the following Movement phase.

    Movement & Momentum

    Select a ship, then click and drag it on the map to set its course — drag distance sets speed, drag direction sets a single course-correction turn (up to the ship's turning stat) toward wherever you drop it. A turn can't begin until the ship has covered its MSMD (Minimum Ship Movement Distance) since the start of the move — 45% of the distance it actually travelled last movement phase, or its fixed Minimum Distance Between Turning stat (Frigate 4", cruisers and Destroyer 8", Heavy Cruiser and Battleship 10") on its very first move, when there's no previous phase to measure yet. The plotted course shows this as an orange stretch where the ship must hold its heading, turning solid white once it's allowed to turn. The reach indicator shown when you select a ship illustrates the same thing directly: a dashed line out from the bow for the MSMD, then the blue turning arc — sized to exactly the one turn a drag can achieve — pivoting from where that line ends. Turning also "costs" extra distance from the movement budget proportional to how sharp the turn is — a hard turn covers less net ground than a gentle one.

    If your intended target isn't reachable in one turn this phase, the ship still turns as hard as it can toward it and covers whatever ground that leaves — the ghost preview always shows exactly where it will actually end up before you confirm.

    Momentum: a ship can't instantly stop or leap to full speed. Your minimum speed this turn is 50% of the distance you covered last turn, and your maximum grows by 25% of that same distance — capped at 300% of the ship's base Movement stat, so top speed keeps climbing the longer you run fast, but levels off rather than compounding forever. (First move of the battle has no floor and uses the base Movement stat as its ceiling.)

    Turret Targeting (part of the Movement phase)

    Every ship's stat window in the Movement phase has a Turret Target box. Pick an enemy from the list (or click an enemy ship on the map) and press Confirm Target. A dashed red line is then drawn from your ship to its target, and when you press End Movement Phase the turret cone swings round to follow that ship while the fleets move, ending up as close to on-target as the turret can manage — by up to the ship's Turret Traverse Speed per turn (Frigate 90°, every other class 60°). Targets stay locked from turn to turn: use Edit Target in any later Movement phase to change one (a target that sinks clears itself). If you pick a target but forget to confirm it, you're warned before the phase ends.

    Until a ship has a confirmed target, its turret cone is stowed relative to the ship: it keeps its angle to the bow and turns with the hull whenever the ship turns, whether that is a course plotted in Movement, a Course Adjustment, or a re-float. Once a target is confirmed the turret is aimed in its own right: it no longer turns with the hull and instead swings toward the target. (What counts is whether the ship has a confirmed target when the Movement phase is carried out. If you press Edit Target, the turret goes back to following the hull.)

    If the turret can't get its cone onto the target in one turn, a small arrow comes off the side of the cone, pointing toward the red line, along with how many degrees of rotation are still needed to bring the line inside the cone. Broadside guns are fixed to the hull and only need the target inside their arc — no aiming needed. Torpedoes aren't aimed at all: they're launched as a Special Attack order in the Special Orders phase.

    Fire Arcs

    Broadside weapons fire into their mounted 90° port/starboard arc. Torpedoes run down a fixed lane straight ahead of the prow: a 7° cone that stops widening once it is 15" across (about 123" out) and then runs on at that width, as a rectangle, out to the torpedo's 600" range. Turrets fire into a 25° arc in front of wherever they're currently facing. Open any of your ships from the fleet list to see its firing arcs painted in red on the map — with the blue movement-reach fan layered on top of them during the Movement phase, so you can plan a move that keeps a target inside (or takes you out of) its own guns' reach.

    The Fleet List

    The draggable window in the corner is a stacked list of every ship you command, with a tick beside each ship that has finished its orders for the phase. Click a ship in the list to open its stat card and orders; click it again (or open a different ship) to close it. Only one ship is open at a time, and while one is open you can't click any other of your ships on the map — you can still drag the open ship to move it, and click an enemy ship to pick it as a target. That keeps crowded formations from fighting over your clicks. With no ship open, clicking one of your ships on the map opens it.

    Shots Per Turn

    Every fitted weapon has a limited number of shots per turn: Cannon and Autogun fire twice, Heavy Cannon (Battleship only) once. Torpedo tubes launch up to twice (as a Special Attack order), but any tube that fired at all is stuck reloading the following turn (no shots at all that turn) before coming back with a fresh two. The Firing and Special Attack panels show a row of circles under each weapon group — filled circles are shots still available this turn, empty ones are already spent (or, for torpedoes, mid-reload).

    Front and rear shots: a ship whose turrets fire at a target in its own front (prow) or rear (stern) arc can only make half its turret shots: each volley at such a target uses up two of every mount's shots for the turn (a mount with a single shot left still fires it). The Fire Control panel warns you when the chosen target is in one of those arcs. Targets on the port or starboard side are unaffected.

    Shields / Armor / Structure

    Each ship has 4 arcs — Prow, Port, Starboard, Stern. Shields (none on the Stern) regenerate at the start of your Movement phase, and absorb hits to that arc until depleted; then Armor absorbs; then Structure takes damage. A ship is destroyed when Structure reaches 0 — or if it sails outside the boundary of the board.

    Critical Damage

    Whenever an enemy weapon's damage gets all the way through to a ship's Structure, that hit gets a Critical Damage check. All of the round's checks are rolled together at the end of the Firing phase. Roll a D20. What it needs depends on how much of the ship's total armor (all four facings added together) is left when the hit lands: 75% or more: 16+; 50% or more: 11+; 25% or more: 6+; below 25% armor, any roll — every hit that reaches Structure is critical. (Torpedoes and mines count too, including a torpedo that hits one of your own ships; running aground does not.)

    A Critical Damage Hit rolls a D6: 1 — catastrophic explosion, the ship is destroyed; 2 to 5 — dead in the water; 6 — steering jammed, and a D2 decides which way (1 = left, 2 = right). If one ship takes several Critical Damage Hits in the same round, its lowest D6 counts, and a ship that is already crippled keeps the worse of the two conditions.

    Dead in the water: in its Movement phase the ship cannot be given movement or turning orders. It still obeys momentum, so it coasts straight ahead at the minimum speed its momentum allows (half of its last move). Steering jammed: you still set the ship's speed (within momentum), but it turns at its full turning stat toward the stuck side, and the turn starts at once — the usual minimum distance before a turn doesn't apply. Guns, torpedoes, mines and Special Orders are not affected.

    Repairs: the effects last the whole Movement phase after the critical hit. At the start of the Movement phase after that, roll a D2: a 2 repairs the ship and it moves normally that phase; a 1 means it is still crippled and the roll is repeated at the start of each following Movement phase until it works. Every roll appears in the log. Each ship that takes a Critical Damage Hit also gets a pop-up box beside it showing the Critical Damage Card (the rolls, what is wrong, and how repairs work); close it with its ×, or it clears when the next Movement phase begins. A crippled ship stays labelled on the map.

    Islands

    Every battle generates 1-5 islands (irregular, randomly shaped, never more than 5% of the board's total area combined). If your plotted course would carry a ship into one, it stops dead the instant it makes landfall — the ghost preview line shows exactly where. Contact knocks off a third of the ship's maximum Structure straight away (bypassing shields and armor entirely), and the ship is grounded: it can't move at all the following turn, then the turn after that you can drag it — up to 30" from where it grounded, shown as a dashed circle — to any position and heading to re-float it, after which it moves normally again. Islands also block line of sight: a shot can't reach a target with land directly in the way, even if the target sits inside the weapon's arc and range. A selected ship's red firing arcs shade dark wherever an island blocks the view through them, and a blocked target is flagged right in the Fire Control target list. While a ship is aground — frozen or waiting to be re-floated, it doesn't matter which — it's an easy target: every attack against it gets +5 on the roll to hit, and its shields don't absorb anything (damage skips straight to armor, the same as a mine). Both go away the instant it's re-floated.

    Gunnery (to-hit)

    Roll 1d20 and add range/sun modifiers: +2 if target is within 1/4 max weapon range, +1 within 1/2 range, −1 beyond 3/4 range, and −5 if aiming within 15° of the Sun. If that total exceeds the firing ship's Gunnery stat, it's a hit. (House ruling: modifiers adjust the roll, not the stat, so "closer = easier" as intended.)

    Special Orders Phase

    After the Firing phase, every ship gets ONE Special Order for the round — a Torpedo Attack, a Lay Mine, or a Course Adjustment — never more than one of the three on the same ship. Pick a button on a ship's panel to choose, and ← Back to change your mind before confirming (this returns to the picker so you can choose a different action instead). Whichever you confirm is locked in for that ship; when everyone's set (or you're happy to leave the rest idle), click End Special Orders Phase. That's the moment everything actually happens — confirmed torpedoes launch, confirmed mines are laid, and their animations play — then the next round begins. Nothing fires or gets laid before you click it, however far along a ship's choice looks.

    Special Orders Phase — Torpedo Attack

    Pick each torpedo a ship should launch (one button per torpedo, up to two per ready tube) and press Confirm Launch. (A ship's confirmed launch shows as a lighter "planned" lane on the map; ships with torpedoes selected but not confirmed won't launch.) Nothing is aimed or rolled at launch: each torpedo simply remembers the spot it was launched from and the lane it was launched into (drawn on the map as a dashed outline, yours in orange and the enemy's in red). Torpedoes strike at the end of the next Movement phase, after every ship has finished moving.

    A ship that has moved out of the lane by then is safe. Of the ships still inside it (and with no island in the way) the torpedo runs into the closest one to where it was launched — friend or foe, so mind your own ships. It then rolls to connect: about 95% point-blank, falling steadily to 10% at the full 600" range, so ships close to the launch are far more likely to be hit. A torpedo that connects does 15 damage through shields, armor and structure like any other hit (the Gunnery stat isn't used). A tube's two shots reload independently — firing only one of them leaves the other ready to fire next phase, while just the fired one spends a round reloading.

    Every ship the torpedo threatened but didn't hit — anyone who was ever in the lane, or within 5" of it, either when it was launched or by the time it resolves — gets a "near miss" Firing Window of its own, even if it was never actually the one rolled against. A ship that dodges clear before resolution still gets this call-out for having been in harm's way at launch; a ship blocked by an island the whole time doesn't.

    Special Orders Phase — Lay Mine

    Frigates may choose Lay Mine as their one Special Order for the phase (this uses up the ship's action for the round — it can't also launch a torpedo or adjust course). Press Lay Mine to stage it, Cancel to back out before the phase ends. The mine is actually dropped just off the ship's stern — and its animation plays — only when you click End Special Orders Phase. Any enemy ship that comes within 10" of a laid mine detonates it automatically (Gunnery 4, Damage 20, ignores shields, hits armor directly).

    Click any mine already sitting on the board to watch it — a Firing Window opens right next to it and loops indefinitely, unlike every other Firing Window (which plays once and closes on its own). It keeps looping until you click something else — a ship, a different mine, or open water — or until that mine is gone.

    Special Orders Phase — Course Adjustment

    Rotate the ship in place, up to half its turning stat either way, with no change to speed or position. Drag the gold handle out from the bow around to the new heading (grayed-out past the limit) and press Confirm Course — it takes effect immediately. Edit Course lets you readjust before the phase ends, still measured from the heading the ship had when the phase began, not from wherever you'd already turned it to.

    Every mechanic above is implemented as literally as the design allowed; the gunnery sign convention was genuinely ambiguous in spec and was resolved with the most game-able, internally consistent reading — ask and it can be tuned.
